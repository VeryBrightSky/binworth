How much is a bin of LEGO worth?

Updated November 15, 2026

Short answer: loose, mixed LEGO sells for about $5 to $12 a pound on eBay (2026 sold listings). The real money is in the minifigures. One figure from the right 2003 set sells for more than 100 pounds of bricks.

How to check your bin in 10 minutes

  1. Dump it on a sheet or towel and pull out every minifigure, plus loose heads, legs and torsos.
  2. Check the details. Printed arms and legs, chrome or mirror finishes, and figures from 2002 to 2010 are where most of the value is.
  3. Look for whole sets. Instruction books, printed pieces, and sets with all their parts sell for far more than loose bricks.
  4. Weigh what's left and use the table above for the brick value.

Where to sell

  • eBay: most buyers, about 13% in fees. Figures one by one, bricks by the pound.
  • BrickLink: where collectors buy single figures. Takes more setup.
  • A local LEGO resale shop: fastest cash, usually a lower price than selling yourself.
  • Facebook Marketplace: good for whole bins, but buyers there know to look for figures too.

Common questions

How much is a bin of LEGO worth?

Loose mixed LEGO usually sells for about $5 to $12 a pound on eBay (2026 sold listings). A 20 lb bin is roughly $100 to $240 as bricks. Rare minifigures inside sell one by one and can be worth more than the whole bin.

How much is LEGO worth per pound?

About $5 to $12 a pound for loose, mixed, used LEGO on eBay in 2026. Clean, sorted bricks and bins with sets or minifigures sell at the top of that range or higher.

Which LEGO minifigures are worth the most?

Among figures that came in regular sets, the 2003 Cloud City Boba Fett (about $1,684 used), Mr. Gold from 2013 Series 10 (about $1,525) and the 2007 chrome gold C-3PO (about $1,193) are the big three. Prices are BrickLink used sale averages via Brickset, November 2026.

Should I sell my LEGO by the pound?

Pull the minifigures out first. Sell the figures one by one and the loose bricks by the pound. Selling a bin with the figures still in it usually leaves most of the money on the table.
